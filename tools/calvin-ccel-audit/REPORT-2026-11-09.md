# Calvin vs CCEL: validation pass and gap list (Nov 9, 2026)

Read-only analysis. Nothing in `library/` was changed. Tool: `tools/calvin-ccel-audit/validate-calvin-ccel.py` (see its README for how to run it).

This replaces the first gap lists written earlier the same day. Those used cruder scans: one counted Scripture blocks and footnotes as missing text (24%), one skipped 10 volumes, and a hand-check wrongly confirmed two paragraphs that are present. Do not cite them.

## 1. Extraction
- 45 volumes, 14,052 scripCom markers (all carry a Bible reference), 13,461 Commentary blocks extracted. The other 591 markers are chapter-heading markers with no commentary right after them (sampled in volumes 8, 22 and 44).
- Scanner defect found and fixed: 169 Commentary blocks (vol 5: 113, vol 6: 43, vol 31: 9, vol 44: 2, vol 3: 1, vol 4: 1) have an empty anchor tag between marker and block, so earlier scans never read them. Now 0 unread blocks.
- Nested divs: none. Two blocks are unterminated in the source (vol 22 Ezek 9:4, vol 28 Mic 6:7): the Commentary div is never closed before `</div2>` or `</div3>`. They caused the earlier Micah false alarms; a block is now capped at the next marker.
- Unmapped references: 0.

## 2. Book and chapter mapping
Each block is assigned from its own OSIS reference and tested against that book only. Multi-book volumes check out (for example vol 28: Jonah 48, Micah 104, Nahum 46 blocks). Every chapter CCEL covers exists in our library.

## 3. Language exclusions
3 paragraphs are excluded as foreign, all Latin (Exod 14, Deut 13). None is English commentary. One uncertain case is kept in the tests.

## 4. Findings
727 paragraphs failed the three-window test (15 none, 35 partial 1 of 3, 677 partial 2 of 3). Re-scored by five-word sequences of the whole paragraph:
- Missing (under 50% covered): 11
- Present, window artefact (85% or more): 689 (Isaiah 519, Acts 157: Hebrew and Greek stored as ASCII transliteration shift the windows)
- Suspect (50-84%): 27 (19 contain Hebrew or Greek script, 8 plain)

Chapter boundary check (first and last qualifying paragraph of each chapter): 12 candidates; 3 real (Gen 1, Isa 19, Lam 5); 7 present at 95-100% (Deut 6, Ps 85, Isa 8, Isa 41, Acts 5, 10, 23); Isa 1 is script-affected; 1 Cor 16 is a missing French gloss.

## 5. Gap list

Confirmed missing (11 paragraphs):
- Genesis 1:1: 2 paragraphs (coverage 6% and 1%)
- Isaiah 19:1: 4 paragraphs (6-10%); our chapter 19 opens at 19:2
- Lamentations 5:22: the last 5 of 6 paragraphs (0-6%); the book ends early, the last missing line is "I will begin tomorrow the explanation of Ezekiel."

Confirmed truncation (1):
- Deuteronomy 16:16: the paragraph "And they shall not appear before the Lord empty..." stops mid-sentence at "was a" and verse 18 follows. About 70 words of its end are missing ("more ancient custom, and common to other nations..."). Found by the 54% score, then read against CCEL.

Suspected, low priority (not all hand-read):
- 1 Cor 16:22: an inline French gloss sentence is absent.
- Exod 10:18, Exod 15:17, Acts 14:14: short tails, 2 to 4 five-word sequences each.
- 12 of the 27 suspects were scored but not read one by one.

Scanner false positives (withdrawn): Isaiah 39:8 (92% present), Acts 6:2 (87% present), Isaiah 26:14, Genesis 47:5, the seven boundary cases above, the Micah/Nahum block (extraction artefact).

## 6. Separate finding: Hebrew and Greek script
Library Isaiah and Acts contain no Hebrew or Greek letters. CCEL's Isaiah volumes have about 8,300 Hebrew and 6,600 Greek letters; its Acts volumes about 100 and 1,800. In our text they appear as ASCII transliteration (CCEL's Hebrew for "bariach" appears in ours as "vrych (bariach)"). The other 46 books keep real script. This breaks the commentary standard's rule that the author's text is unchanged, and it can be recovered from CCEL.

## 7. Open decisions
- Editor notes: CCEL carries 6,553 "- Ed" footnotes; our library has almost none. They are the editor's words, not Calvin's. Proposed: leave them out of the commentary, or keep them in a separate, labelled editorial field.
- Ezekiel "chapter 21" in our library is editor and translator material; CCEL's Ezekiel ends at chapter 20.
- Repair: a recorded, reversible rule in `library/fixes.json` for the 11 paragraphs and the Deuteronomy 16:16 tail, and a separate decision on restoring Hebrew and Greek in Isaiah and Acts.

## Limits
This audit checks presence and coverage, not exact equality. A paragraph can be fully covered and still sit in the wrong place. The CCEL volumes are a derived digital edition; the independent print witness would be the Calvin Translation Society editions (1844-1856).
