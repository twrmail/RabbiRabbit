John 3:18

He who believes in him is not judged. He who doesn’t believe has been judged already, because he has not believed in the name of the only born Son of God.

Rabbit's question: Condemned already: what does that mean?

John Calvin · The word · Calvin commentary JHN
Modern language, by AI
John Calvin says the words condemned already stress that unbelievers are utterly ruined. He adds that Christ speaks especially of people whose wickedness shows itself in open contempt for the gospel.
John Calvin in his own words
The past tense of the verb, is condemned already , ( ἤδη κέκριται ,) was used by him emphatically, ( ἐμφατικῶς ,) to express more strongly that all unbelievers are utterly ruined. But it ought to be observed that Christ speaks especially of those whose wickedness shall be displayed in open contempt of the Gospel.
Jamieson, Fausset & Brown · The word · Commentary Critical and Explanatory on the Whole Bible
Modern language, by AI
Jamieson, Fausset and Brown say the believer has passed from death to life the moment he believes. The one who is condemned already is the one who rejects the single way out that God gave his Son to provide, and so chooses to stay condemned.
Jamieson, Fausset & Brown in his own words
18. is not condemned—Having, immediately on his believing, "passed from death unto life" (Joh 5:24).
condemned already—Rejecting the one way of deliverance from that "condemnation" which God gave His Son to remove, and so wilfully remaining condemned.
Matthew Henry · A warning · Matthew Henry's Commentary on the Whole Bible
Modern language, by AI
Matthew Henry reads here the fate of those who will not believe in Christ: they are already condemned. Their sin is serious because of the one they dismiss, the only Son of God, who is completely true and good and deserves to be believed and embraced. God sent the one dearest to himself to save us, so shouldn't he be dearest to us?
Matthew Henry in his own words
(1.) Read here the doom of those that will not believe in Christ: they are condemned already. Observe, [1.] How great the sin of unbelievers is; it is aggravated from the dignity of the person they slight; they believe not in the name of the only-begotten Son of God, who is infinitely true, and deserves to be believed, infinitely good, and deserves to be embraced. God sent one to save us that was dearest to himself; and shall not he be dearest to us?

John 3:19

This is the judgment, that the light has come into the world, and men loved the darkness rather than the light, for their works were evil.

Rabbit's question: Whose fault is the darkness?

John Calvin · Where teachers differ · Calvin commentary JHN (this note covers 19-21)
Modern language, by AI
John Calvin says people think it harsh that those who do not believe in Christ are doomed. So that no one blames Christ for his own condemnation, John shows that each person should blame himself, since unbelief is a sign of a bad conscience. It is their own wickedness that keeps unbelievers from coming to Christ.
John Calvin in his own words
All think it harsh that they who do not believe in Christ should be devoted to destruction. That no man may ascribe his condemnation to Christ, he shows that every man ought to impute the blame to himself. The reason is, that unbelief is a testimony of a bad conscience; and hence it is evident that it is their own wickedness which hinders unbelievers from approaching to Christ.
Matthew Henry · A warning · Matthew Henry's Commentary on the Whole Bible
Modern language, by AI
Matthew Henry says humanity's fall began in a desire for forbidden knowledge and is kept alive by a desire for forbidden ignorance. People love their sickness and their slavery and will not be made free or whole. The true reason they love darkness is that their deeds are evil: they think darkness excuses their deeds, and they hate the light because it takes away the good opinion they had of themselves by showing them their sin and misery.
Matthew Henry in his own words
Man's apostasy began in an affectation of forbidden knowledge, but is kept up by an affectation of forbidden ignorance. Wretched man is in love with his sickness, in love with his slavery, and will not be made free, will not be made whole. [3.] The true reason why men love darkness rather than light is because their deeds are evil. They love darkness because they think it is an excuse for their evil deeds, and they hate the light because it robs them of the good opinion they had of themselves, by showing them their sinfulness and misery.
Jamieson, Fausset & Brown · What it means · Commentary Critical and Explanatory on the Whole Bible
Modern language, by AI
Jamieson, Fausset and Brown say this is the condemnation in a strong sense: it reveals a condemnation that already exists and seals in it those who will not be delivered from it. The light has come into the world in the person of Jesus himself. They add that loving darkness can only be known by deliberately rejecting Christ, but that rejection reveals it in a frightening way.
Jamieson, Fausset & Brown in his own words
19. this is the condemnation, &c.—emphatically so, revealing the condemnation already existing, and sealing up under it those who will not be delivered from it.
light is come into the world—in the Person of Him to whom Nicodemus was listening.
loved darkness, &c.—This can only be known by the deliberate rejection of Christ, but that does fearfully reveal it.
John Chrysostom · Background · Homily on the Gospel of John 28
Modern language, by AI
Chrysostom points out that Jesus takes away every excuse when he says the light came into the world. Did they seek it themselves, he asks, or work to find it? The light itself came to them, and even so they would not hurry toward it.
John Chrysostom in his own words
And observe how in another way He deprives them of all excuse, when He saith that, "the light came into the world." "Did they seek it themselves," He saith, "did they toil, did they labor to find it? The light itself came to them, and not even so would they hasten to it."

John 3:20

For everyone who does evil hates the light and doesn’t come to the light, lest his works would be exposed.

Rabbit's question: What does the light expose, and why does that feel threatening?

Matthew Henry · What it means · Matthew Henry's Commentary on the Whole Bible
Modern language, by AI
Matthew Henry puts it simply: Christ is hated because sin is loved. People who do not come to the light show a secret hatred of it; if they did not resist saving knowledge, they would not sit so contentedly in ruinous ignorance.
Matthew Henry in his own words
Christ is hated because sin is loved. 3. They who do not come to the light thereby evidence a secret hatred of the light. If they had not an antipathy to saving knowledge, they would not sit down so contentedly in damning ignorance.
John Calvin · What it means · Calvin commentary JHN
Modern language, by AI
John Calvin explains that the light is hateful to such people only because they are wicked and want to hide their sins. By rejecting the remedy, they deliberately feed the very ground of their condemnation. He warns that we are mistaken if we think those who rage against the gospel are moved by godly zeal; they actually avoid the light so that they can flatter themselves more freely in the dark.
John Calvin in his own words
20. For whosoever doeth what is evil. The meaning is, that the light is hateful to them for no other reason than because they are wicked and desire to conceal their sins, as far as lies in their power. Hence it follows that, by rejecting the remedy, they may be said purposely to cherish the ground of their condemnation. We are greatly mistaken, therefore, if we suppose that they who are enraged against the Gospel are actuated by godly zeal, when, on the contrary, they abhor and shun the light, that they may more freely flatter themselves in darkness .
John Gill · The word · An Exposition of the Old and New Testament
Modern language, by AI
John Gill says the person who does evil avoids the light for fear his deeds will be exposed, that he will be shamed and blamed, and that he will be told to give them up, which he does not want to do.
John Gill in his own words
lest his deeds should be reproved; or discovered, and made manifest, and he be brought to shame, and laid under blame, and advised to part with them, which he cares not to do; see Ephesians 5:11.
Jamieson, Fausset & Brown · The word · Commentary Critical and Explanatory on the Whole Bible
Modern language, by AI
Jamieson, Fausset and Brown define reproved here as exposed by detection.
Jamieson, Fausset & Brown in his own words
20. reproved—by detection.

John 3:21

But he who does the truth comes to the light, that his works may be revealed, that they have been done in God.”

Rabbit's question: What does coming to the light look like?

Jamieson, Fausset & Brown · What it means · Commentary Critical and Explanatory on the Whole Bible
Modern language, by AI
Jamieson, Fausset and Brown describe the one who does truth as someone whose only aim in life is to be and do what can stand the light. He loves the light and comes to it so that everything about him, thoroughly tested, may be seen to be divinely wrought and divinely approved.
Jamieson, Fausset & Brown in his own words
21. doeth truth—whose only object in life is to be and do what will bear the light. Therefore he loves and "comes to the light," that all he is and does, being thus thoroughly tested, may be seen to have nothing in it but what is divinely wrought and divinely approved. This is the "Israelite, indeed, in whom is no guile."
Matthew Henry · The teaching · Matthew Henry's Commentary on the Whole Bible
Modern language, by AI
Matthew Henry describes the character of a good man: he does truth, acting sincerely in all he does. He may sometimes fall short of the good he wants to do, and he has his weaknesses, but he aims honestly and holds on to his integrity. And he is one who comes to the light.
Matthew Henry in his own words
Observe here, 1. The character of a good man. (1.) He is one that doeth truth; that is, he acts truly and sincerely in all he does. Though sometimes he comes short of doing good, the good he would do, yet he doeth truth, he aims honestly; he has his infirmities, but holds fast his integrity; as Gaius, that did faithfully (3Jo 1:5), as Paul (2Co 1:12), as Nathanael (Joh 1:47), as Asa, 1Ki 15:14. (2.) He is one that cometh to the light.
John Calvin · The word · Calvin commentary JHN
Modern language, by AI
John Calvin says Christ uses the word truth because we are easily fooled by the outward shine of good works and do not look at what is hidden inside. Upright people, free from hypocrisy, willingly come into the presence of God, who alone is the fit judge of our works. Works are called done in God when he approves them and they are good by his standard.
John Calvin in his own words
Christ employed the word truth , because, when we are deceived by the outward lustre of works, we do not consider what is concealed within. Accordingly, he says, that men who are upright and free from hypocrisy willingly go into the presence of God, who alone is the competent Judge of our works. For those works are said to be done in God or according to God , which are approved by Him, and which are good according to His rule.
Augustine · Living it · Tractates on John 12 (this note covers 19-21)
Modern language, by AI
Augustine says we begin to do the truth when we stop soothing and flattering ourselves and stop calling ourselves righteous while we are not. Coming to the light means letting our works be shown for what they are. Even the displeasure we feel at our own sin is God's doing: it would not trouble us unless God shone into us and his truth showed it to us.
Augustine in his own words
Thou doest the truth, and comest to the light. How is it thou doest the truth? Thou dost not caress, nor soothe, nor flatter thyself; nor say, "I am righteous," whilst thou art unrighteous: thus, thou beginnest to do the truth. Thou comest to the light, that thy works may be made manifest that they are wrought in God; for thy sin, the very thing that has given thee displeasure, would not have displeased thee, if God did not shine into thee, and His truth show it thee.
